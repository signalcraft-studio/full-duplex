Roaming vs travel eSIM vs satellite: what actually works abroad

What each option does, what it costs UK travellers and where it works.

By Sam Beer · 19 October 2026 · 8 min read

Share on X

Will my phone work when I land, and what will it cost? There are now three answers, and they do different jobs. Roaming keeps your own number and plan working abroad. A travel eSIM is a cheap data line you add for the trip. Satellite-to-phone is a safety net for places with no mast at all. It isn't a way round roaming charges.

In eleven years inside mobile networks, most of the bill shocks I've seen came from people mixing these up. Here's what each does today.

1. Roaming: your own SIM, someone else's network

When you roam, your phone uses a partner network abroad and your home operator bills you. You keep your number, so calls, texts and bank codes arrive as normal.

Inside the EU, "roam like at home" is the law for EU customers: calls, texts and data are charged at domestic prices, subject to a fair use policy[1]. Since Brexit, UK customers no longer have that protection, so each UK operator sets its own terms. Here is what the big four publish today:

  • O2 includes roaming in its Europe Zone on its tariffs at no extra cost. If your UK allowance is over 25GB, you can use up to 25GB in the zone, after which you need a data Bolt On[2].
  • EE charges £2.72 a day to use your allowances in its European zone on plans that started on or after 7 July 2021, and nothing extra on plans that started before that date[3]. Its EE plan terms set a 50GB fair use limit for data used in its Europe zone if your UK allowance is bigger than that, so check your own plan's terms[4]. Its current price guide lists a weekly EU pass at £16.50 and a 15-day pass at £30[5].
  • Vodafone charges £2.75 a day in its Europe zone (Zone B) if your plan doesn't include European roaming. It also sells an 8-day pass for £16 and a 15-day pass for £21[6]. A 25GB roaming fair use limit applies, and Ireland, the Isle of Man, Iceland and Norway (Zone A) are included[7].
  • Three charges £2.75 per 24 hours in its "Go Roam in Europe" destinations if you joined or upgraded on or after 18 December 2025, and £2 if you joined or upgraded between 1 October 2021 and 17 December 2025. Up to 12GB of your data allowance works in Europe. If you have a Spend Cap, it must be high enough to cover the charge or roaming won't work[8].

Further afield it jumps: Vodafone charges £8 a day in its Zones C and D[9]. Three charges £8 a day on newer plans[10]. EE's rest-of-world passes are £6 or £8 a day, depending on the zone[5].

Roaming is for trips, not living abroad. O2 treats more than 63 days of inclusive roaming in any four-month period as unfair use and can add surcharges after sending you a warning text[11]. Vodafone's equivalent is more than 62 days, or more use abroad than at home, over four months[7].

Two protections help: your provider must text you on arrival explaining charges and limits (unless you've opted out), and if you set a bill limit, it can only charge beyond it with your agreement[12]. Ofcom also warns that phones can roam by accident near borders, for example between Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland[12].

Insider tip: your plan's start date matters as much as the network. Two people on the same operator can pay different roaming charges.

2. Travel eSIM: a second, data-only line for the trip

An eSIM is a SIM built into the phone that you download instead of inserting. A travel eSIM is a prepaid data plan you add next to your normal line. You install it by scanning a QR code or through an app, and you need Wi-Fi to download it[13][14].

Three things decide whether it works for you:

  1. Your phone must support eSIM. On iPhone that means an iPhone XS, XS Max, XR or later[14]. MoneySavingExpert says most Pixels and iPhones have supported eSIM since around 2018, and Samsung flagships since 2020. You can check by dialling *#06#: if an EID number shows, the phone should be compatible[13].
  2. Your phone must be unlocked. Apple says your iPhone can't be locked to a network if you want to use another provider abroad. Check under Settings > General > About for "No SIM Restrictions"[14]. UK operators have been banned from selling locked handsets since 17 December 2021[15]. Older or second-hand phones are worth checking.
  3. You don't get your UK number on it. Worldwide travel eSIMs are generally prepaid data plans, and Apple separates them from local operators' plans, many of which do come with a local number[14]. Your UK number stays on your normal SIM. If you leave both lines on, the travel eSIM carries your data but "you might still incur roaming fees from your home eSIM", in Apple's words[14]. With the home line off, iMessage and FaceTime still work on your home number.

On cost, Which? checked prices on 1 July 2026 for a 10-day trip to ten popular destinations outside the EU. The cheapest 5GB plan it found ranged from £5.99 to £13.63 depending on the country, and the cheapest unlimited plan from £15.15 to £27.00. Some prices were converted from dollars. Prices change and vary by destination and length of stay[16]. Ten days of roaming at £8 a day is £80.

Where it falls down: bank codes still come to your UK number, so if you need them, leave the home line on and accept that its roaming rules apply.

3. Satellite-to-phone: a safety net with borders

The newest and most misunderstood option. These services connect an ordinary phone to a satellite when there's no mobile signal. Today they carry texts and a few light apps, not general browsing, and each is licensed country by country.

  • O2 Satellite (UK) is a £3-a-month Bolt On for pay monthly customers with a compatible phone. It covers texting, location sharing and apps such as WhatsApp, Messenger and maps. Standard voice calls, including emergency calls, aren't supported. O2 says it is "only available in the UK for now", and it isn't designed for use on planes or at sea[17].
  • T-Mobile T-Satellite (US) covers the continental US, Puerto Rico, Hawaii and parts of southern Alaska. Through partners it also covers Canada (Rogers), Australia (Telstra), New Zealand (One NZ) and Japan (KDDI). It supports texting, picture messages, location sharing and selected apps. People who aren't T-Mobile customers can add it for $10 a month on an unlocked, eSIM-capable phone. T-Mobile's support page doesn't say whether you can sign up without a US account or address, so don't plan a trip around it[18].
  • Apple Emergency SOS via satellite is built into iPhone 14 and later and lets you text emergency services when you have no mobile or Wi-Fi signal. It works in 21 countries, including the UK, the US, Canada, Australia, Japan and much of western Europe. Visitors can use it there too, unless their iPhone was bought in certain countries such as mainland China or Russia[19]. It's free for two years after you activate an iPhone 14 or later[19].
  • Google Pixel Satellite SOS works on Pixel 9 and later (not the 9a) in the US, Canada, Puerto Rico, Australia and a list of mostly European countries[20].

We couldn't find any operator saying whether visitors on a foreign SIM can use its satellite service, so don't count on it. So for a British traveller: O2 Satellite stays at home, and the emergency SOS features travel with you only to countries where they're licensed. One catch: on ships and planes, services delivered via satellite systems fall outside EU roaming rules and have no price caps, so the EU advises turning roaming off or using flight mode on board[1].

When to use each

  • A week in Spain or France: check your plan. On O2 it's included; on a daily-charge plan, compare a pass with a travel eSIM.
  • Two weeks in the US, Thailand or Japan: at £6–£8 a day, roaming adds up. A travel eSIM for data plus your UK line for texts is the usual combination.
  • Hiking or driving out of signal: download what you need first, check whether your phone's satellite SOS is licensed there, and don't expect O2 Satellite to follow you abroad.

Whichever you choose, read your operator's roaming page the week you travel. Three and EE build yearly rises into their roaming prices[8][5].

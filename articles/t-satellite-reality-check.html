T-Satellite a year on: lifeline, not a replacement

By Sam Beer · Published 15 October 2026 · 1 min read

Share on X

T-Mobile launched T-Satellite with Starlink commercially in July 2025. It works on most modern smartphones and covers parts of the US with no cell towers.[1] T-Mobile says more than 150,000 people use it every day, and it now carries data for a set of satellite-ready apps including WhatsApp, Google Maps and AllTrails.[1]

Where it proved itself: disasters

T-Mobile says T-Satellite supported more than 250,000 users and 1.5 million messages during Winter Storm Fern, and delivered more than 100 Wireless Emergency Alerts. It is also expanding through reciprocal roaming in Canada and New Zealand.[2]

Where it hasn't (yet): everyday traffic

CEO Srini Gopalan has said satellite accounts for about 0.0002% of T-Mobile's network usage, and only 0.0003% in peak summer months. He calls it a 'complementary' service that will become 'table stakes'.[3] He also confirmed that the exclusivity deal with Starlink does not end in 2026. T-Mobile is working on a D2D joint venture with AT&T and Verizon.[3][4]

The Full Duplex view

Direct-to-cell is becoming a standard safety net, much as roaming once did. The bigger question is who controls the satellite layer, and that is what the carrier joint venture is about.

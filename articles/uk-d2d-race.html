Starlink vs AST SpaceMobile: the UK's two-horse race for satellite-to-phone

VMO2 picked Starlink. VodafoneThree chose AST SpaceMobile.

By Sam Beer · 15 October 2026 · Updated 23 November 2026 · 2 min read

Share on X

Two of the UK's big mobile groups have chosen different satellite partners.

Virgin Media O2 + Starlink

VMO2 launched O2 Satellite in February 2026 using Starlink Direct to CellDirect to cell Starlink’s name (and the industry’s shorthand, D2C) for satellites that act as a mobile mast in space and connect to normal 4G phones through a partner network. VodafoneThree + AST SpaceMobile

In July, Vodafone said UK beta testing would start at the beginning of 2027, once AST had about 45 satellites in orbit — the number Vodafone said it needed to start.[5] That slipped an earlier plan for customer trials in summer 2026 through Satellite Connect Europe, a Vodafone Group and AST SpaceMobile joint venture. The aim is still a service capable of data, voice and SMS.[4] Ofcom approved a licence variation in April 2026 allowing VodafoneThree to provide D2DD2D Direct to device: a satellite talking straight to an ordinary phone, with no dish or special handset. Your phone thinks it has found another mast, just one a few hundred kilometres up. Glossary services in its 900 MHz spectrum, and handset exemption regulations for that band took effect on 10 July 2026.[3] It builds on Vodafone's January 2025 video call made directly to an AST BlueBird satellite from rural Wales.[4] AST has since moved its target for about 45 satellites in orbit to mid-2027. It still expects operator beta trials early in 2027, and says those work best with about 25 satellites.[7][8] The 45-satellite milestone is the same one other partners are waiting on: Telus completed a first network integration test with AST in October 2026.[6]

Why the band matters

Lower frequencies such as 900 MHz generally travel further and get into buildings better than higher ones such as 1800 MHz, although capacity and satellite design matter too. O2 Satellite is already live on 1800 MHz; VodafoneThree's AST service on 900 MHz is still ahead of its UK beta. Worth watching how the two bands compare once both are serving customers.

Inner Signal: the life of a phone call

A woman in Warminster rings her son’s mobile from her own phone. He is hiking in the Highlands, miles from the nearest mast. We shrink down and ride the call, from the moment she presses call to the line on her bill.

Written by a mobile network insider with over 13 years in GSMGSM Global System for Mobile communications: the 2G standard from 1991 that started it all, and the base that SMS, roaming and SIM cards were built on. Glossary, from IVR and SMS to call routing and thin and thick MVNOsMVNO Mobile virtual network operator: a brand that sells mobile service over someone else’s network. A thin MVNO mostly resells; a thick MVNO runs its own core network, SIMs and billing. Glossary.

An honest note. As of November 2026, the only live UK satellite-to-phone service is Virgin Media O2’s O2 Satellite service, over Starlink Direct to CellDirect to cell Starlink’s name (and the industry’s shorthand, D2C) for satellites that act as a mobile mast in space and connect to normal 4G phones through a partner network. Glossary. It offers texts, location sharing and a short list of apps, including WhatsApp. WhatsApp calls can work, but they are internet (VoIP) calls over the app’s data, not a normal phone call, so quality varies and they can drop as satellites pass. Standard phone calls, including 999, are not supported yet. This story’s satellite voice leg is the near future being built, not something you can dial today.

Chapter 1 of 14 · Her network

She presses call

In a kitchen in Warminster, she types her son’s mobile number and presses call. We shrink down and climb aboard the Call Pod. Her phone is already on a normal UK mast, so the first hop is a short radio hop: the number, and a request to place the call, go up to the lighthouse on the hill.

  • Call Pod

Radio · call setup

Phone  →  mast (BTS / eNodeB)   radio signalling
   GSM-style:  CM Service Request  (MO call), then  SETUP
              with calledPartyBCDNumber = 07700 900123
   Today’s VoLTE:  SIP  INVITE  to her IMS core (via the P-CSCF)
              Request-URI / To: +447700900123
   07700 900000 to 900999 is Ofcom’s reserved drama range
  • On a mobile she does not send each digit as a musical tone, the way a landline does. The whole number travels in the setup message once she presses call.
  • 07700 900000 to 07700 900999 is the mobile range Ofcom sets aside for TV and drama, so no real person will ever answer it.

How it used to work

If she had placed this call from a landline, the first hop would have been a copper wire into a local exchange, which would have handed the call to a transit switch and then to the mobile operator. The UK’s old analogue phone network, the PSTN, is due to go digital on 31 January 2027. After that, a “landline” call is really a broadband call that arrives at the same mobile front door.

The switchboard operator hands the baton to today’s MSC signalman. The job is the same: take the call, and send it on.

Up the mast

Our lighthouse keeper takes the Call Pod in from the air and sends it down a cable, into her operator’s core. From here the call is a visitor in a very large, very tidy machine.

Chapter 3 of 14 · Her network

“Prove you’re you”

The network must be sure her secret agent is genuine, without ever asking for the secret itself. In a live network this check usually ran when her phone last signed on, and it can run again when she starts a call. We show it here so you can see it happen. The network sends a random number, the RAND. Inside her SIM, the glowing key (Ki) scrambles it into an answer (SRES). In the network’s AuC vault, the twin key has already worked out the right answer. If they match, the agent is real. Both sides also make a one-off ciphering key, and her conversation is scrambled from here on. Ki itself never leaves either vault. Over the air she wears her TMSI disguise, not her real name.

  • Her IMSI
  • TMSI mask
  • Ki
  • AuC vault guardian

GSM security · DTAP (or AKA in 4G/5G)

VLR → phone   Authentication Request   RAND (128 bit)
Her SIM        SRES′ = A3(Ki, RAND);   Kc = A8(Ki, RAND)
phone → VLR    Authentication Response   SRES′
VLR            SRES′ = SRES ?   yes →  Cipher Mode Command
  • An eSIMeSIM A SIM built into the phone that you download instead of inserting. Handy for adding a second line, such as a satellite plan, without swapping cards. Glossary is only a downloadable profile. It still holds the IMSI and the Ki, so the same authentication applies whether the profile arrived on a plastic SIM or over the air.
  • Newer networks (3G, 4G, 5G) also make the network prove itself to the phone. Original GSM only checked the phone.
  • The TMSI is a temporary mask, handed out by the VLR and changed regularly, so one hidden number cannot be followed from place to place.

Chapter 4 of 14 · Her network

The bouncer at the door

She is the one placing the call, so she is the one who pays. Before the call is allowed out of her network, the charging system is asked: is this call allowed, and is there credit? The question travels over CAMEL, a rulebook that lets a prepaid platform step into any call. Credit is fine, so the bouncer waves the Call Pod through. If she had been out of credit, the journey would have ended here, with a short recorded message instead of a ring.

  • Charging bouncer
  • Her IMSI

CAMEL · CAP (or online charging over Diameter)

Originating MSC → SCP   CAP  InitialDP
   serviceKey, callingPartyNumber, calledPartyNumber (07700 900123),
   imsi (hers), eventTypeBCSM = collectedInfo
SCP → charging   balance and rate check  (a UK-to-UK mobile call, plus any satellite surcharge)
SCP → MSC       CAP  Continue     (or ReleaseCall to refuse, ApplyCharging to set a time limit)
  • This is why the credit check sits on her side. In the UK the caller usually pays. Receiving a call on your home network is typically free, though there are exceptions, for example if you are roamingRoaming Using another network when yours isn’t there, usually abroad. Satellite-to-phone works much like roaming: your phone attaches to a partner network that happens to be in space. Glossary abroad and someone rings you.
  • On a 4G or 5G network the same job is done by online charging over Diameter, talking to a CHF or OCS. The bouncer is the same, the doorway is newer.
  • SCP stands for Service Control Point, the “brain” that runs prepaid rules. This is where a lot of real operator money is made and lost.

Chapter 5 of 14 · The join

Across the interconnect

Her signalman sees a number that belongs to another operator (people can switch network and keep their number, so he has to check). He hands the Call Pod over at the interconnect, the official join between the two companies. Waiting at the far door is the passport officer: his network’s Gateway MSC, which takes calls arriving from the outside world. The message that does the handing over is called an IAM.

  • MSC signalman
  • Gateway MSC passport officer
  • Call Pod

SS7 · ISUP (or SIP at the interconnect)

Her MSC/GMSC  →  his Gateway MSC    ISUP  IAM  (Initial Address Message)
   Called party number  : +44 7700 900123
   Calling party number : her mobile (presentation allowed)
   Transmission medium  : speech
   sent at a Point of Interconnect
  • On today’s UK networks this would usually be a 4G or 5G voice call (VoLTE), handled by an IMS core rather than a classic MSC. The jobs are the same: check who you are, check you can pay, and hand the call to the other operator. We keep the older names here because they make the roles easier to follow.
  • ISUP is the call-setup language of the old telephone network. It runs over SS7 signalling links, and today often over IP as SIP-I or native SIP.

Chapter 6 of 14 · His network

“Where is he?”

A Nokia 3310, still in the archive. It would like you to know it also holds a very good game of Snake.

The Gateway MSC has a phone number but no idea where the phone is. It asks the HLR, the network’s master register of subscribers. The archivist takes the public name (the MSISDN, which is simply the phone number) and finds the secret agent behind it: his IMSI, the identity that lives in his SIM. The number everyone knows is not who he really is. His agent steps out of the archive and joins the story.

  • His IMSI
  • Call Pod

   interrogationType : basicCall

Chapter 7 of 14 · His network

A room key for one call

The archivist knows which area his phone last reported in, but that is not a number you can ring. So she asks the visitor register there (the VLR) for a temporary routing number made for this one call, the MSRN. The receptionist cuts a hotel room key that works once, then is useless. The HLR passes it back, and the Gateway MSC sends the Call Pod on towards it.

  • VLR receptionist
  • His IMSI

Chapter 8 of 14 · Space

The twist: there is no mast

Here is the clever bit. Her son is in a glen with no ordinary mobile signal, but a satellite overhead is acting as a mobile mast in space: our orbiting lighthouse. The Call Pod rides a beam from a ground stationGround station A dish site on Earth that links satellites to the internet and to the mobile network’s core. With a bent-pipe satellite, your call or message comes down here before it goes anywhere else. Glossary (the gateway) up to the satellite, which relays it down to his phone on ordinary mobile frequencies. His phone treats it as an ordinary cell.

An honest note. As of November 2026, the only live UK satellite-to-phone service is Virgin Media O2’s O2 Satellite service, over Starlink Direct to Cell: texts, location sharing and a short list of apps, including WhatsApp. WhatsApp calls can work, but they are internet (VoIP) calls over the app’s data, not a normal phone call, so quality varies and they can drop as satellites pass. Standard phone calls, including 999, are not supported yet. VodafoneThree has said its planned AST SpaceMobile service is expected to include voice, with beta testing expected in early 2027. This leg of the story shows that near future, not a native voice call you can place over satellite today.

Shouting his disguise across the glen

Now the network has to find him. It calls out his temporary disguise, the TMSI, across the whole satellite beam, which is far bigger than a normal mobile cell. A disguise matters: shouting his real identity over a wide area would let anyone with a radio follow him around. Only one phone in the glen recognises the mask, and it answers.

“Prove you’re you,” again

His network now runs the same check she passed, with his own agent and his own key. A new random challenge goes out. Inside his SIM, his Ki scrambles an answer. The twin in his operator’s AuC vault already knows what the answer should be. They match, a fresh ciphering key is made, and his radio link is locked. Two keys, two vaults, two agents: neither secret has crossed the air.

  • His IMSI
  • Ki
  • AuC vault guardian

  • The two authentications are independent. Her network proved she is she; his network now proves he is he. Neither operator has to trust the other’s key.

Chapter 11 of 14 · His network

Border control for the ship

The agent checks out, but what about the ship he is travelling in? Every phone has a unique 15-digit serial number, the IMEI, stamped on its hull. Many operators then ask the EIR, the border-control register, and the sniffer dog is sent out. The check is optional and depends on the operator’s policy. In this story the hull number is not on the stolen-phone list. Green light: cleared to proceed.

  • IMEI
  • EIR sniffer dog

SS7 · MAP

MSC → phone   DTAP  Identity Request (IMEI)
phone → MSC   Identity Response   IMEI 35 123456 654321 1   (illustrative)
MSC → EIR     MAP  CheckIMEI (imei)
EIR → MSC     equipmentStatus = whiteListed
  • The first 8 digits say which make and model (the TAC), the next 6 are the serial number, and the last is a check digit.
  • When the check is done, the EIR can return white, grey or black list. Stolen phones are often added to a shared industry list, so a phone reported stolen on one UK network can be blocked on others. Not every network checks the IMEI on every call.

Chapter 12 of 14 · The join

Ring ring: the agents meet

Meter 4:12 call connected, charging

Often, end to end: AMR12.2 kbit/s

Only if the join needs it: G.71164 kbit/s

His phone buzzes in his pocket. A message called ACM (Address Complete) races all the way back to her network, which plays her the ringing tone. He answers, and a second message, ANM (Answer), tells every exchange along the route that the call is connected. At that moment the meter starts, and the two secret agents meet in the middle: her IMSI and his IMSI, shaking hands over a living call.

Now her voice is on the move. Because both ends are mobiles, it can often stay in the compact mobile format (AMR) the whole way. It only gets transcoded into the old 64 kbit/s telephone format if the join between the two operators still needs it. Up at the satellite, travelling at about 17,000 mph, the network constantly adjusts timing and frequency to cancel the Doppler effect: the same trick that makes an ambulance siren change pitch as it passes.

  • Her IMSI
  • His IMSI
  • Call Pod

ISUP · DTAP

phone → MSC   DTAP  Alerting         →  ISUP  ACM  back to her network  →  ringback tone
phone → MSC   DTAP  Connect          →  ISUP  ANM  →  charging starts
SCP           CAP  EventReportBCSM (oAnswer)  →  her prepaid timer begins
Voice path    AMR end to end where the interconnect allows it;
              otherwise transcoded (AMR ↔ G.711) at a media gateway
Satellite     typical low orbit a few hundred kilometres up, speed about 7.5 km/s (about 17,000 mph);
              Doppler and timing advance pre-compensated (3GPP NTN, Release 17 onwards)
  • No ANM means no billing. Until someone answers, the call is free, which is why a lot of fraud tries to get calls “answered” without a person.
  • Standard GSM timing only copes with distances up to about 35 km. Satellite systems need extra timing tricks, which is part of what the NTN standards add.

Chapter 13 of 14 · Back office

Hang-up, and a line on two bills

Her bill07700 9001234 min 12s

When she says goodbye and hangs up, a REL (Release) message goes hop by hop along the route, and each exchange answers RLC (Release Complete) once it has let go of the line. Behind the pod the lights go out, one stop at a time. Then the afterlife begins. Both networks write a small receipt, a CDR (call detail record): hers for the call she placed, his for the call he received. The receipts drift to mediation, where they are tidied and merged, then to rating, which puts a price on them, and to billing. The two operators also settle up with each other, because her operator pays his operator a small fee for delivering the call. The grave is a line on her bill.

  • Billing accountants
  • Her IMSI
  • His IMSI

SCP            →  CAP  EventReportBCSM (oDisconnect), final charge reported

  • For prepaid there is no monthly bill. The record feeds the charging system that already took the money while she talked.
  • Two receipts for one call is normal. Each operator only sees its own half clearly, which is why mediation and wholesale billing exist.

Alternate ending: nobody answers

Tech support desk, circa 2007. “Have you tried turning it off and on again?” An in-joke from the author’s days on the support floor.

In another universe he is deep in a peat bog with his phone in his bag, and does not answer. After 20 to 30 seconds of ringing, the network’s no-reply timer runs out and the call is quietly diverted to voicemail. A friendly robot voice (the IVR) asks her to leave a message after the tone. If his phone had been out of reach altogether, the network would have diverted her at once, and she would never have heard a ring.

  • Call Pod
  • HLR archivist

ISUP · call forwarding

GMSC/MSC   no-reply timer (NRy, typically 20 to 30 s) expires
HLR profile  call forwarding on no reply → voicemail access number
ISUP IAM     redirecting number = his number, redirection reason = no reply  →  voicemail platform
IVR          plays greeting, records message
Voicemail    SMS to him: “You have a new voicemail”   (MAP MT-ForwardSM)
  • The “not reachable” case (switched off, no signal at all) is a separate setting and diverts immediately.
  • IVR means Interactive Voice Response: any computer that talks to callers and listens to their key presses or voice.
  • When voicemail answers, the network treats the call as connected (an ANM is sent). Whether she is charged, and how much, depends on her tariff: some UK tariffs make voicemail free or cheap, others apply a short rate.

What comes next

Today’s crew still does the work you have just ridden through. These characters are a look at what is arriving, or still expected, around that same journey.

  • In the UK, Virgin Media O2’s O2 Satellite service is already live for texts, location sharing and some apps, over Starlink Direct to Cell. A native voice call to an ordinary phone, of the kind this story rides, is the next step being built, using 3GPP Non-Terrestrial Networks from Release 17 onwards.

  • Those NTN standards give the radio extra help with long delay and Doppler shift, so a phone in a glen can treat a fast-moving satellite as a cell. Later 3GPP releases add further enhancements.

  • AI-driven networks are being trialled and are emerging. They are not deployed everywhere, and this character stands for that work in progress, not a system you will find in every core today.

  • An eSIM is a downloadable profile. It still holds the IMSI and the Ki, so the same authentication you met in this story applies, whether the profile arrived on a plastic SIM or over the air.

  • 3GPP’s Non-Terrestrial Networks work, from Release 17 onwards, is how ordinary phones and satellites are being standardised to talk to each other. 6G is expected around 2030, as 3GPP studies the groundwork in Releases 20 and 21. That date is an expectation, not a fact.

  • Many designs still bring the call down through a ground station before it reaches the core. The keeper is expected to remain part of the path, even as more of the radio moves into orbit.

The whole trip in one breath

One tap on a mobile, two networks, two secret agents who meet in the middle, a satellite moving at 17,000 mph, and a pair of billing systems that would not give up. It all took a few seconds to connect and left a single line on her bill: 07700 900123, 4 min 12s.

Standard voice calls from an ordinary UK phone, over a satellite, are not here yet. Virgin Media O2’s O2 Satellite service is live for texts, some apps, and WhatsApp calls over the app’s data. Other operators have native voice in their plans. Signal is where we follow what comes next.

Where the details come from
  • Call setup and routing: ITU-T Q.763 (ISUP message formats) and Q.764 (ISUP procedures); 3GPP TS 23.018 (basic call handling) and TS 29.002 (MAP).
  • Mobile-originated prepaid and CAMEL: 3GPP TS 23.078 and TS 29.078 (CAP). On a mobile-originated call the usual trigger is DP Collected_Info, at the originating MSC. 4G and 5G online charging uses Diameter or a CHF, with the same job.
  • Numbers and identities (IMSI, TMSI, IMEI, MSRN, MSISDN): 3GPP TS 23.003. Drama numbers: Ofcom’s reserved range 07700 900000 to 900999.
  • GSM security (RAND, SRES, Kc, A3, A8, A5): 3GPP TS 43.020. 4G and 5G use AKA to do the same job, in TS 33.401 and TS 33.501.
  • VoLTE / IMS: 3GPP TS 23.228. We keep the older MSC and HLR names in the story because the roles are easier to follow; today’s UK voice calls usually run this way over IMS.
  • Satellite access to ordinary phones: 3GPP Non-Terrestrial Networks, Release 17 onwards, including radio adaptations for long delay and Doppler shift. Later releases add further enhancements.

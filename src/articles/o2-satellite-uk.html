On 26 February 2026, Virgin Media O2 switched on O2 Satellite, a satellite-to-mobile service powered by Starlink Direct to Cell. It made VMO2 the first operator in the UK and Europe to launch satellite-powered data services, aimed at 'not-spots' with no terrestrial coverage.[1]

How the regulation came together

Ofcom published a framework for authorising direct-to-device (D2D) services in mobile spectrum in December 2025. Ookla describes it as the earliest in any European country and notes that it broadly follows the FCC's Supplemental Coverage from Space model.[2] Ofcom approved VMO2's licence variation in February 2026. Licence-exemption regulations took effect on 25 February 2026, which allowed existing handsets to connect.[3]

Spectrum and price

The service runs over O2's licensed 1800 MHz spectrum, which is transmitted from Starlink's low-Earth-orbit satellites. At launch it cost £3 a month on Pay Monthly plans and came at no extra cost on 'Ultimate' tariffs.[2] Starlink says customers get data, voice and video through apps, plus messaging, in remote areas.[1]

Why it matters

This is the first time a normal phone on a UK network can fall back to a satellite without special hardware.[2] For operators, coverage stops being only about masts.

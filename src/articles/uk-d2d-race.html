Two of the UK's big mobile groups have chosen different satellite partners.

Virgin Media O2 + Starlink

VMO2 launched O2 Satellite in February 2026 using Starlink Direct to Cell over 1800 MHz spectrum.[1][2]

VodafoneThree + AST SpaceMobile

UK beta testing is now planned for the beginning of 2027, once AST expects to have about 45 satellites in orbit — the number Vodafone says it needs to start.[5] That slips an earlier plan for customer trials in summer 2026 through Satellite Connect Europe, a Vodafone Group and AST SpaceMobile joint venture. The aim is still a service capable of data, voice and SMS.[4] Ofcom approved a licence variation in April 2026 allowing VodafoneThree to provide D2D services in its 900 MHz spectrum, and handset exemption regulations for that band took effect on 10 July 2026.[3] It builds on Vodafone's January 2025 video call made directly to an AST BlueBird satellite from rural Wales.[4] AST's ~45-satellite target for early 2027 is the same fleet milestone other partners are waiting on: Telus completed a first network integration test with AST in October 2026.[6]

Why the band matters

Lower frequencies such as 900 MHz generally travel further and get into buildings better than higher ones such as 1800 MHz, although capacity and satellite design matter too. O2 Satellite is already live on 1800 MHz; VodafoneThree's AST service on 900 MHz is still ahead of its UK beta. Worth watching how the two bands compare once both are serving customers.

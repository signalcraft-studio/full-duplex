What happens when you hit send

You’re on a hillside with no signal. You type a text and press send. Here’s the trip it takes, in six short scenes.

Written by a mobile network insider with over 13 years in GSM, from IVR and SMS to call routing and thin and thick MVNOs.

  1. 1 / 6

    No signal on the hill

    Your phone loses signal on a hillside. The nearest mast is too far away, or the hill is in the way.

  2. 2 / 6

    A mast in space

    It finds a satellite overhead. The satellite acts like a mobile mast in space, so a compatible phone connects with your normal SIM and number. No app, no special handset.

  3. 3 / 6

    Down to Earth

    The satellite relays your text down to a ground station, sometimes hopping across other satellites by laser first.

  4. 4 / 6

    Into your network’s core

    From the ground station it goes into your mobile network’s core, the same system that handles every text. That’s where it’s billed to your plan, where an emergency text picks up location information, and where routing works out where it’s going.

  5. 5 / 6

    Across to their network

    Your network looks up who the number belongs to and hands the text across to the other person’s network, the same way as any other text.

  6. 6 / 6

    Delivered, and handed over

    Their phone buzzes. Meanwhile your satellite, moving at about 27,000 km/h, is already sliding towards the horizon, so the next one takes over.

Good to know

  • Texts first. Most satellite-to-phone services today handle texts, plus a few apps in some places. Calls and fuller data are coming as more, bigger satellites go up.
  • You need the sky. It works outdoors with a clear view of the sky, and it can take a little longer than a normal text.
  • It depends on your network. Your mobile operator needs a satellite partner, and your phone and plan need to support it. See who’s live where.
